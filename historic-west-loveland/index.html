What City Records Show

Two Historic Districts, Two Different Eras

The City of Loveland has designated both West 4th Street and West 5th Street as local historic districts. They sit close together west of downtown, but their houses come from different decades.

According to the city, the West 5th Street district was approved by City Council in April 2009 and covers 25 properties. Its survey area spans parts of 13 small additions platted between 1883 and 1919. Of 51 inventoried houses, 22 were built in the 1900s and 9 in the 1920s.

West 4th Street is newer. Its homes went up in the 1920s in the Clearview 2nd Addition, platted in 1919. Contractor William W. Green built them, mostly as one-story buff brick bungalows.

Why does that matter for plumbing? Homes built across several decades are rarely identical inside, because owners remodel at different times. Two neighbors on one block can have very different pipe, drain and water heater setups. We do not guess what is behind your walls. A technician looks at the pipe, fixture or drain in front of them and explains what they found before work starts.

Practical Guidance

Older Homes, Honest Expectations

A house from 1905 and one from 1925 can have very different plumbing, and either may have been updated. The only reliable way to know is to look. Water heater age, visible pipe material in the basement or crawl space, and past drain backups are the first clues. If you just bought here, our older home plumbing inspection checklist covers what to ask before small problems grow.

Mature trees line many older Loveland streets. Roots can enter any sewer lateral that has a gap or crack, whatever the age of the house. When several fixtures drain slowly or back up together, a camera inspection shows what is happening inside the line. That is faster than guessing, and it tells you whether drain cleaning is enough or the line needs sewer line repair.

If your home sits inside a designated district, ask the city historic preservation office whether a project needs review before you change anything visible from the street. We can describe what a plumbing job involves, so you know what to ask.

When To Call

Signs An Older Home Needs A Plumber

Several Drains Slow At Once

This often points to the main line, not a single fixture.

Rust Colored Or Weak Water

Discolored water or low pressure can mean worn supply pipe or a failing valve.

Stains On Ceilings Or Walls

Brown rings or damp drywall can mark a slow leak behind the surface.

An Aging Water Heater

Tank units often last 8 to 12 years, so an older one deserves a check.

Plumbing work in Colorado follows the state plumbing code, and some jobs, such as a water heater replacement or sewer repair, can require a permit. We tell you what applies to your project before work starts.

Before We Arrive: Active Leak Or Burst Pipe In Your Older Home

  • Shut off the main water valve first. In older homes it is often in a basement, crawl space or utility closet.
  • If water is near an outlet, switch or panel, stay away from that area and touch nothing electrical.
  • Move valuables out of the wet area if it is safe to do so.
  • Call our 24/7 emergency line and tell the dispatcher your street and what you see.
