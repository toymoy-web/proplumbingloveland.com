What This Covers

Restoration, Not Just The Repair

Water damage restoration is its own service category, separate from fixing whatever caused the water in the first place. If a burst pipe soaked a basement, our plumbing side repairs the pipe, while the restoration crew handles extracting standing water, drying out the structure, and preventing the mold growth that follows if moisture sits inside walls and flooring for more than a day or two. Both sides are coordinated on the same call, so nothing falls through the gap between "the leak is fixed" and "the water damage is actually gone."

Loveland sits close enough to the Big Thompson River corridor that local homeowners are generally more aware of water-related risk than most markets, and that awareness is well placed for a different reason than most people assume. The bigger day-to-day risk isn't the river itself. It's routine mechanical failures inside the home: a supply line that lets go behind a washing machine, a water heater tank that finally ruptures, or a sump pump that can't keep up during a fast snowmelt. Those events account for the large majority of the restoration calls we actually run, not flooding from the river.

Before Help Arrives: What To Do

  • If it's safe, shut off the water source, the fixture's supply valve or the home's main shutoff.
  • Do not use household electrical outlets or appliances in a standing-water area.
  • Move furniture, rugs, and electronics away from the wet area if you can do so safely.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup starts. It helps document the loss for your insurer.

Restoration work generally follows the IICRC's S500 water damage standard, which is what sets the actual drying benchmarks, moisture-reading targets, and equipment placement guidance the industry uses rather than just running fans until things look dry. That standard is also what most insurance adjusters expect a restoration report to reference, which matters if you end up filing a claim.

Timing matters more than most homeowners expect. Materials that stay wet past roughly 48 hours are past the point where drying alone reliably prevents mold growth, which is the real reason we treat this as a 24/7 emergency service rather than something that can wait for a normal business-hours appointment.
