The Community

A Planned Neighborhood With Its Own Metro District

The Lakes at Centerra sits in northeast Loveland within the larger Centerra development. According to the district itself, The Lakes at Centerra Consolidated Metropolitan District, a local special district, finances and maintains public improvements there. That includes Explorer Park, Frank Farms Park and parkway landscaping, paid for through homeowner property assessments.

Homes were built in phases by several builders, and listings describe single-family houses, townhomes and condos. Build years differ by section, so we do not assume one age for the whole neighborhood. Tell the dispatcher what you own and roughly when it was built.

Practical Guidance

Townhomes, Condos And Shared Walls

If you own a townhome or condo, find your unit's main shutoff before you need it. Ask your HOA which pipes, drains and fixtures are yours and which belong to the association. A leak in one unit can reach the neighbors, so acting quickly matters.

Newer homes tend to have different calls than older ones. Water heater trouble, fixture leaks, jammed disposals and hard water scale are common starting points. We diagnose first and do not guess. If water spots and scale keep returning, read our note on hard water in Loveland or look at water softener service.

Where private responsibility ends and a utility or district begins can differ by address. Your water bill, HOA papers and the metro district can tell you who maintains what. Once we see the problem, we can tell you what sits on your side of the line.

Townhome and condo owners often wonder whether to call the HOA or a plumber first. If water is actively flowing, stop it at your shutoff, then call us and your HOA contact. For a slow drip or a recurring clog, a plumber can usually tell whether the cause is inside your unit or in a shared line. That makes the HOA conversation shorter and more useful.

Water heaters in townhomes and condos often sit in a garage, closet or utility room. Keep the area around the unit clear. If you see moisture or a drip near the relief valve line, have it checked soon, because it can point to a pressure or temperature problem.

Plumbing work in Colorado follows the state plumbing code, and some jobs, such as a water heater replacement, can require a permit. We tell you what applies before work starts.
