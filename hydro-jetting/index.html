Why Snaking Alone Sometimes Isn't Enough

Punching Through Vs. Scouring Clean

A drain snake is built to break through or hook whatever's blocking the pipe and pull it out or push it along, which works well for a one-time clog. What it doesn't do is clean the pipe wall itself. Grease, scale, and root fragments that have built up along the inside of the pipe mostly stay in place around wherever the snake cut a path through. That's exactly why the same drain tends to clog again a few weeks or months later in the same spot.

Restaurants and other commercial kitchens in the Loveland area are where we see the most extreme version of this pattern, since grease accumulates far faster in a commercial line than a residential one. A grease trap that isn't being pumped and maintained on schedule pushes that problem downstream into the building's drain lines, where regular snaking becomes a losing battle against buildup that keeps re-forming. Jetting on a set schedule, rather than only after a backup happens, is the more realistic long-term answer for that kind of line.

How It Works

Camera First, Then Jetting

Before jetting any line with unknown condition, we run a camera inspection to confirm the pipe is sound enough to handle the pressure. A cracked, bellied, or severely deteriorated section can be damaged by jetting, which is exactly why that check comes first rather than jetting blind. Once the line is confirmed, a jetting nozzle sends high-pressure water through the pipe, scouring grease, scale, and debris off the interior wall rather than just clearing a path through the middle of it.

Grease And Roots

Jetting For Downtown Kitchens And Mature Lots

Restaurants and commercial kitchens in Downtown Loveland and around the Promenade Shops at Centerra send a lot of grease down their lines, and hydro jetting is the standard way to clear it. At home, repeat clogs on older streets near Lake Loveland are more often roots in a clay or cast iron lateral. A camera pass first tells us whether jetting will actually solve the problem.

Before You Book

Questions Callers Ask About Jetting

Most jetting calls begin after a failed attempt. A cable went in, the water drained for a few weeks, and the clog returned. Others stalled at a certain length and the technician gave up. Both stories point toward a different tool, and the dispatcher should hear them.

A Clog Every Two Or Three Years

A line that clogs on a schedule is collecting buildup faster than a cable can clear it. Jetting cleans the full pipe wall, so the gap between clogs usually grows. Roots are the exception because they regrow, and a line with root damage may still need a repair after the cleaning.

A Kitchen Line Or The Whole Main

Kitchen branch lines carry grease and food debris, so they clog earlier than bathroom lines. A main sewer line carries everything at once and is harder to reach. A cleanout makes access easier and the job easier to price. Routine blockages that do not need jetting are covered on the drain cleaning page.

Reading An Estimate

Price depends on line length, access, and how heavy the buildup is. Ask whether the quote includes a camera look before and after, since the footage shows what the jetting actually changed.
