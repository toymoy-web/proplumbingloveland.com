Signs Of A Sewer Line Problem

Multiple Slow Drains

Soggy Or Sunken Yard Spot

Diagnosis First

Why We Camera-Inspect Before Quoting

Guessing at a sewer line problem leads to unnecessary digging, which is expensive and disruptive for no reason. A camera run through an existing cleanout shows the actual condition of the pipe, root intrusion, a cracked joint, a bellied section holding standing water, or a fully collapsed run, and exactly where it sits relative to the house and yard. That footage is what the repair quote is based on, not a guess.

Loveland's older neighborhoods near downtown are the areas where we see this most, since clay and cast iron sewer laterals installed decades ago are far more prone to root intrusion and joint separation than the PVC used in newer construction. A line's age and material alone don't tell us what's wrong, though. Two identical-age clay lines can be in very different condition depending on soil movement and nearby tree root activity, which is exactly why the camera inspection comes before any quote rather than pricing off an assumption based on the neighborhood.

Any pipe material, slope, and connection method used in the repair follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standard Colorado has adopted, which is what actually governs how a lateral is allowed to tie back into the existing line and the city sewer main.

Repair Methods

Trenchless Lining Vs. Excavation

A damaged section a few feet long is often a good candidate for trenchless pipe lining, which rehabilitates the inside of the existing pipe through an access point without trenching the yard open. A fully collapsed section, a severe misalignment, or damage close to the foundation sometimes still needs a small targeted excavation instead. Either way, this is a spot repair, fixing the damaged section while leaving the rest of a sound line in place, which is different from replacing the whole line end to end.
