How Often to Flush a Tankless Water Heater in Loveland

Most tankless water heater guides recommend an annual flush. In Loveland, that advice is usually too conservative, and here's the real reason why.

By the Loveland Pro Plumbing team

If you've searched for tankless water heater maintenance, you've probably seen the same advice everywhere: flush it once a year. That's a reasonable default for most of the country, but it's built around average water hardness, and Loveland's water isn't average. Front Range surface water carries enough dissolved calcium and magnesium that a tankless unit here scales up meaningfully faster than the same unit running on softer water elsewhere.

Why Tankless Units Are More Sensitive To This Than Tank Units

A standard tank water heater has a large reservoir, so mineral sediment settles across a wide surface area and takes years to become a real problem. A tankless unit works differently. Every gallon that passes through gets forced across a small, narrow heat exchanger, and that's exactly where scale accumulates fastest. A tank can often go a decade before sediment becomes a serious issue. A tankless unit in hard water can show real performance loss within a single year if it's never flushed.

Signs You're Overdue For A Flush

Rather than going strictly by the calendar, watch for the actual symptoms. Water that starts lukewarm instead of hot, a noticeable delay before hot water reaches the tap, or a unit that clicks and short-cycles are all classic signs of scale restricting flow through the heat exchanger. If you notice any of these before a year has passed since the last flush, that's a sign your specific water usage and hardness level are pushing you toward a more frequent schedule than the generic advice assumes.

Does The Brand Of Your Unit Change Anything?

The core maintenance need is the same across brands, since it's driven by water chemistry, not the specific manufacturer. What does vary is the exact flush procedure and any brand-specific service ports or isolation valve kits, which is one more reason a professional flush tends to go more smoothly than a first attempt at a DIY version on an unfamiliar unit.

What An Actual Flush Involves

A proper flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit's heat exchanger, usually for 45 minutes to an hour, dissolving mineral buildup that's accumulated since the last service. It's not the same as simply running hot water through the tap, and it's not a job most homeowners have the isolation valves and pump setup to do themselves without some plumbing knowledge. Our tankless water heater service covers exactly this, along with a full inspection of the venting and igniter while the unit is already open for service.

What Happens If You Skip It

Ignoring scale buildup doesn't just mean lukewarm showers. Left long enough, it forces the unit to work harder to reach target temperature, which shortens the realistic lifespan of the heat exchanger and can eventually trigger error codes that look like a bigger failure than they actually are. A surprising number of the tankless service calls we run in Loveland turn out to be exactly this: a maintenance issue that looks like appliance failure until someone actually opens the unit up.

A Practical Schedule For Loveland Homes

For most households here, flushing every 8 to 10 months is a more realistic interval than the standard annual recommendation, especially for a family of four or more running the unit daily. A single-person household with lighter usage might reasonably stretch closer to the standard annual mark. If you're not sure which category your household falls into, the symptoms above are a better guide than trying to calculate an exact interval yourself.

If You're Also Dealing With Hard Water Elsewhere In The House

A tankless flush addresses the symptom on that one appliance, but if you're also seeing spots on dishes, scale around faucets, or dry skin after showers, that's the broader hard water problem showing up across the house, not just the water heater. A water softener addresses the mineral content at the source, which reduces how often the tankless unit needs attention in the first place rather than just treating one symptom at a time. Homeowners in nearby Berthoud deal with the same Front Range water hardness we see in Loveland, so the same maintenance schedule applies there too.

What Actually Happens Inside The Unit

Scale doesn't build up evenly. It concentrates on the hottest surfaces inside the heat exchanger, since mineral solubility drops as water temperature rises, which means the same water that would carry those minerals through a cooler pipe drops them right where the unit is doing the most work. Over time that buildup acts like a layer of insulation between the heating element and the water passing through it, forcing the unit to run longer and hotter to hit the same output temperature. That's the mechanical reason lukewarm water and clicking sounds show up together. The unit is compensating for reduced heat transfer, not simply malfunctioning.

Cost Of A Flush Versus Cost Of Neglect

A routine flush and descale service is a modest, predictable cost. Letting scale buildup go unaddressed for multiple years tends to end one of two ways: a heat exchanger that fails early and needs replacing, which is a meaningfully larger repair than routine maintenance would have been, or a unit that limps along at reduced output for years without anyone realizing maintenance was the actual fix all along. Neither outcome is necessary, and both are avoidable with a flush interval that actually matches your water hardness and usage rather than a generic national default.

Common Questions About Tankless Maintenance

Can I flush the unit myself? Technically possible with the right isolation valves and a small pump, but most residential installs don't come with an easy DIY setup, and getting the descaling solution concentration and circulation time wrong can leave scale only partially removed.

Does a flush void my warranty if I don't use the manufacturer's exact process? Most manufacturers require documented periodic maintenance to keep a warranty valid, which is worth checking against your specific unit's documentation, but a professional flush using standard descaling solution and proper isolation valve technique satisfies this for the large majority of residential tankless brands.

Will a flush fix a unit that's already showing an error code? Often, yes, if the code is related to flow restriction or overheating protection, both common symptoms of scale buildup. Codes related to ignition or electrical faults are a different issue and need separate diagnosis.

How long does a professional flush actually take? Most residential flush and inspection visits run 45 minutes to an hour and a half, depending on how much scale has built up and whether the visit also includes checking the igniter, venting, and error history while the unit is already open.

Does hard water shorten a tankless unit's overall lifespan even with regular flushing? Regular flushing largely offsets the extra wear from hard water, which is why keeping to schedule matters more here than in a soft-water region. A well-maintained tankless unit in Loveland can still reach a comparable service life to one running on softer water elsewhere.
