Garbage Disposal Not Working? Try This Before You Call

Before scheduling a repair, a few quick checks resolve a surprising number of garbage disposal problems on their own.

By the Loveland Pro Plumbing team

A garbage disposal that suddenly stops working is one of the more common calls we get, and it's also one of the easier problems to at least partially diagnose yourself before deciding whether you need a technician. Here's what's actually worth checking first, in order.

First: Is It Humming But Not Spinning?

A humming sound with no grinding action almost always means the flywheel is jammed on something the disposal can't grind, a fruit pit, a bone fragment, or a utensil that fell in by accident. Turn off the disposal at the wall switch first. Never put your hand inside the disposal to check for a jam. Most units have a small hex-shaped opening at the bottom center, accessible from underneath the sink, where you can insert an Allen wrench and manually work the flywheel back and forth to dislodge whatever's stuck.

Second: Is It Completely Silent?

No sound at all, not even a hum, often points to a tripped reset button rather than a dead motor. Most disposals have a small red or black reset button on the bottom of the unit. Pressing it firmly can restore power if the unit tripped due to overheating or a temporary overload. If the reset button doesn't hold, or trips again immediately after resetting, that's a sign of a more significant electrical or motor issue rather than a simple overload.

Third: Check The Circuit Breaker

Before assuming the disposal itself has failed, check whether the circuit breaker controlling that outlet has tripped, especially if other kitchen outlets on the same circuit have also lost power. This is a simple check that's easy to overlook when the obvious suspect is the appliance itself.

What About A Leak Underneath?

A leak is a different problem from a jam or power issue, and it's not something you can fix with a reset button or an Allen wrench. Leaks typically trace back to a worn seal around the sink flange where the disposal connects to the sink drain, or a cracked housing after the unit has taken a hard jam one too many times over its life. Both require an actual repair rather than a homeowner fix, since accessing and replacing either the flange seal or the unit itself involves disconnecting plumbing connections.

When These Steps Don't Work

If clearing a jam with the hex wrench doesn't restore normal operation, if the reset button won't hold, or if you're dealing with a leak, that's the point where professional disposal repair becomes the right call rather than continuing to troubleshoot. A technician can also tell you whether the unit is worth repairing at all or has reached the point where replacement makes more sense, typically once a disposal has failed more than once within a year or the motor itself has burned out.

What Not To Do

Never reach into a disposal with your hand, even when it's switched off, since some units retain enough residual charge or accidental switch contact risk to make this genuinely dangerous. Never pour drain cleaner into a disposal that's jammed, since chemical drain cleaners don't address a mechanical jam and can create a hazardous situation if a technician later needs to work on the unit by hand. Most manufacturers' own safety guidelines specifically warn against both of these, which is worth checking if you still have your unit's documentation.

Preventing The Next Jam

Most jams come from a short list of repeat offenders: fibrous vegetables like celery, fruit pits, bones, and utensils that slip in accidentally while washing dishes. Running cold water while the disposal operates, rather than hot, also helps solid grease stay firm enough to grind through cleanly instead of coating the blades and housing.

Is Your Kitchen Sink Involved Too?

A garbage disposal issue sometimes shows up alongside a slow-draining kitchen sink, especially if food debris has built up in the drain line beyond the disposal itself. If clearing the disposal jam doesn't fully restore normal draining, drain cleaning for the line downstream of the disposal may be the remaining piece of the problem. This is common enough in older homes around Loveland and nearby Windsor that we treat a slow kitchen drain and a disposal complaint as related, not separate, issues worth checking together.

Bottom Line

A humming or silent disposal is often fixable in under five minutes with a reset button press or a hex wrench. A leak, a jam that won't clear, or a unit that keeps tripping the same way repeatedly are the situations actually worth a service call, and knowing the difference saves you from either an unnecessary appointment or an unsafe DIY attempt.

How Long Should A Disposal Last?

Most residential garbage disposals last somewhere between 8 and 12 years with normal use. A unit within that range that's jammed once and cleared easily is usually fine to keep using. A unit at or past that age that's needed the same fix more than once in the past year, or where the motor itself sounds strained even after clearing an obvious jam, is a better candidate for replacement than continued repair.

A Few Habits That Extend Disposal Life

Running the disposal with a steady stream of cold water, both during and for a few seconds after grinding, helps flush ground material fully through the trap rather than letting it settle. Avoiding fibrous foods like celery and corn husks, along with bones and fruit pits, prevents the most common jam causes before they happen. Occasionally grinding ice cubes can help dislodge minor buildup on the grinding components, though it's not a substitute for a proper flange seal check if you're already seeing signs of a leak.

What About Odors Coming From The Disposal?

A persistent odor without any mechanical problem is usually trapped food debris in the splash guard or the disposal chamber itself rather than a sign of failure. Grinding a few ice cubes along with a small amount of citrus peel can help clean and freshen the chamber. A stubborn odor that doesn't clear with regular cleaning sometimes points to buildup further down the drain line, which brings the disposal and drain issues discussed above back together as one underlying cause rather than two separate ones.

Is It Worth Upgrading When You Replace?

If you're already facing a replacement rather than a repair, it's worth considering whether your current unit's horsepower actually matches your household's usage. A unit that was already underpowered for a busy kitchen will keep jamming under the same conditions even after replacement with an identical model. Stepping up to a higher horsepower unit at replacement time is a reasonable, one-time upgrade that can meaningfully reduce future jam frequency.

Whichever situation you're in, the underlying goal is the same: fix the actual cause rather than repeatedly clearing the same symptom. A jam that keeps recurring in the same way is telling you something about either usage habits or the unit's capacity, and addressing that root cause saves more time over a year than any individual quick fix.
