From Farm Town To Front Range Growth Story

Timnath's Recent History

Timnath was a quiet agricultural community built around potatoes, alfalfa, sugar beets, and cattle for most of its history after being platted in 1882. That changed fast. The town's population grew from roughly 625 residents in 2010 to over 11,000 today, almost entirely due to its location near the Harmony Road and I-25 interchange. Old Town Timnath, with its original town hall and fire station on a bluff above the Cache la Poudre River, is still there, now surrounded by new subdivisions on nearly every side.

Median household income in Timnath now runs well above most of the surrounding towns. That shows up in the size and finish level of the homes we're called out to, larger primary suites, multiple bathrooms, and higher-end fixtures than the older farmhouses the town was originally built around. Practically none of that newer housing carries the aging-pipe risk we watch for elsewhere in Larimer County. The plumbing calls we see most in Timnath's newer neighborhoods trace back to fixture quality, water heater capacity for larger households, and irrigation systems that came with the property.

Timnath's median home value has climbed past $780,000 in recent estimates, among the highest of any town in this part of Larimer County. That lines up with what we see on site: larger square footage, multiple water heaters in some homes, and irrigation systems extensive enough that a backflow preventer and its annual test are a routine part of property upkeep rather than an afterthought.

Old Town Timnath itself is worth a specific mention for anyone in that small original core rather than the surrounding new subdivisions, since it's the one part of town where older pipe material genuinely shows up in our service calls. Everywhere else, the newer construction that's defined Timnath's growth over the past decade means the town's plumbing profile looks closer to Johnstown's than to an older, established Front Range town.

Timnath's location right at the Harmony Road and I-25 interchange has made it one of the more convenient stops on our regular route between Loveland and the towns further north. That keeps response times consistent even as the town keeps adding new subdivisions on what used to be open farmland along that corridor.
