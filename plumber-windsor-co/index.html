Old Town Vs. The New Subdivisions

Two Very Different Parts Of Town

Old Town Windsor, near Windsor Lake and Boardwalk Park along the Poudre River, grew up around the Great Western Sugar Company's plant starting in the early 1900s. Some of that original housing stock is still standing today. The rest of Windsor tells a completely different story. The town's population has grown from under 20,000 in 2010 to nearly 44,000 today, almost entirely in new subdivisions with modern PEX plumbing. A plumbing call near Old Town is more likely to involve aging pipe material. A call from the newer growth areas is more likely to be a fixture, water heater, or appliance connection issue.

Windsor's split between Larimer and Weld counties is more than a line on a map for plumbing purposes too, since permit and inspection processes technically run through whichever county a given property sits in, even though the town itself operates as one municipality. We handle that distinction as a matter of routine rather than something the homeowner needs to sort out. Windsor Lake itself sits close enough to some of the town's oldest streets that homes in that immediate area were generally built well before the newer growth on Windsor's outer edges. That's usually the first thing worth knowing about a specific property's likely plumbing age.

Windsor runs its own Weld RE-4 school district rather than sharing one with a neighboring town, which reflects how much the town has grown into its own identity even as it keeps expanding. Windsor High School's enrollment has grown enough in recent years to justify a major building expansion, a useful proxy for just how much new housing, and new plumbing, has gone in around it over the same stretch of time.

The Poudre River corridor running through town adds its own consideration for properties closest to the water, generally higher groundwater and a greater chance of drainage or sump pump questions coming up during a service call than on a lot farther from the river. That's a real, checkable difference between a home near Windsor Lake or Boardwalk Park and one out in the newer subdivisions on higher, drier ground toward the edges of town.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Windsor

  • Stay away from any outlet, switch, or panel that's anywhere near the water.
  • Clear the immediate area of anything that can be moved safely.
