Filtration Vs. Softening

Two Different Problems, Not One

Filtration and softening get lumped together, but they solve different problems and often work best paired rather than as either-or. A softener is specifically built to remove the calcium and magnesium that cause hardness, scale, and the mineral buildup covered elsewhere on this site. A filter is built for taste, odor, chlorine, sediment, and specific contaminants, none of which a softener's resin bed is designed to touch. A household with both hard water and taste concerns typically benefits from both systems working together, not a single unit trying to do both jobs.

Sediment is worth a specific mention for older Loveland homes on original supply lines, since aging galvanized pipe can shed small particles of rust and scale into the water over time. A whole-home sediment filter catches that before it reaches fixtures and appliances, which is a different, more mechanical problem than the taste and odor issues a carbon filter is built to address.

Reverse osmosis deserves a specific mention too, since it's the most thorough option available for a single tap. It forces water through a semi-permeable membrane that removes a much broader range of dissolved substances than a standard carbon filter. That's why it's the usual choice when a household wants the highest level of treatment specifically for drinking and cooking water rather than every fixture in the house.

System Types

Whole-Home Vs. Under-Sink

Whole-home filtration installs at the point where water enters the house, treating every fixture at once, showers, laundry, and every tap. Under-sink filtration treats water at one specific location, most commonly the kitchen, and is where reverse osmosis systems typically go for the highest level of treatment on drinking and cooking water specifically. Many households run both, whole-home for general use and a dedicated under-sink system for drinking water.

Taste And Sediment

Filtration Choices For Different Homes

Loveland's treated water comes largely from Front Range mountain supplies, including the Big Thompson River, and the complaints we hear most are chlorine taste, cloudy water after line work, and sediment in older homes. An under-sink filter at the kitchen tap is plenty for most newer homes in Centerra, while an older house near downtown with aging galvanized pipe may need whole-home sediment filtration to protect fixtures.
