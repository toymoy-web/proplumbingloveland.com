The Real Problem

A Repair Doesn't Fix Failing Pipe Material

Most homeowners call about the symptom directly in front of them, a leak under the kitchen sink, then a different leak in the bathroom wall a few months later, treating each as its own unrelated repair. When those calls keep coming from the same house, the actual problem usually isn't any single fitting or joint. It's the pipe material itself, most often aging galvanized steel, corroding from the inside out along its entire length. Fixing the leak in front of you doesn't touch the corrosion happening everywhere else in that same pipe run, which is why the leaks keep showing up somewhere new.

Homes built roughly before the 1970s are the ones we see this in most often around Loveland, since galvanized steel was the standard supply pipe material through that era before copper and later PEX took over. A galvanized line doesn't fail all at once. It narrows gradually as rust builds up on the inside of the pipe wall, which is why falling pressure often shows up gradually over a year or two rather than as a single sudden event, and why it's easy to attribute to something else at first.

Full Vs. Partial Repipe

Whole-Home Or Just The Worst Sections

A full repipe replaces every supply line in the home with new PEX piping, which is the right call when galvanized pipe throughout the house is showing the same age and corrosion pattern. A partial repipe, replacing just the worst-affected sections, sometimes makes sense when the rest of the home's piping is a newer material or in noticeably better condition, though it doesn't eliminate the chance of another failure in an untouched section down the line.
