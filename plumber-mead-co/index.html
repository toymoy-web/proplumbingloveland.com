A Railroad Town With A Small Old Core

Mead's Old Town And What It Means For Plumbing

Mead's post office opened in 1907 and the town incorporated on March 17, 1908, named for pioneer settler Dr. Mead as the railroad came through. The original grid around Third Street, where Town Hall still sits at number 441, holds homes from the early and middle 1900s. Those houses are where the older-pipe calls come from, galvanized supply lines, aging cast iron drains, and original sewer laterals that have outlasted their design life.

Almost everything else in Mead is recent. The population was about 2,000 in 2000, about 3,400 in 2010, and 4,781 in 2020, a 40 percent jump in the last census decade alone. Most of that housing is newer subdivision construction on the flat farmland around the old town, so calls there tend to look like Johnstown's more than Berthoud's. Think water heater sizing, fixture warranty issues, and softener installs rather than failing galvanized pipe.

Getting There

The I-25 And Highway 66 Corridor

From our Loveland office the route is I-25 south to the Highway 66 exit, roughly 15 miles and a straight interstate run. Mead sits in Weld County, so permits and inspections follow the Town of Mead and Weld County rather than Loveland's city rules. Our technicians handle that paperwork on permitted jobs like water heater replacements and gas line work.

Mead is served by St. Vrain Valley Schools, which is why many addresses around town carry a Longmont mailing address even though the home sits in Mead. Mead High School opened in 2009, and its own address is Longmont ZIP 80504. If your mail says Longmont but your neighbors say Mead, call us anyway and we will confirm coverage from the address.
