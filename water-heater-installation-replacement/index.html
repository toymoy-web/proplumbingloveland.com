Why Loveland's Water Matters Here

Hard Water And Tank Lifespan

Front Range water carries enough mineral content to build sediment inside a standard tank noticeably faster than in soft-water regions. That sediment layer insulates the tank bottom from the burner, which is where the rumbling and popping sound comes from, and it shortens the realistic service life of an unmaintained tank. A yearly flush slows this down, but most tanks that reach 10-12 years without regular flushing are already showing corrosion by the time a homeowner calls about a leak.

Every install we complete follows the plumbing code framework Colorado has adopted from the International Plumbing Code, which covers things like temperature and pressure relief valve routing, combustion air for gas units, and seismic strapping on tank units. None of that is optional, and skipping it is how a home fails a resale inspection years later even if the water heater itself works fine in the meantime. We handle the permit and inspection side as part of the install rather than treating it as an upsell, so there's nothing left for a home inspector to flag down the road.

Tank Vs. Tankless

Which Fits Your Home

Traditional Tank

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Simpler installation
  • Limited hot water supply
  • 10-15 year lifespan

Tankless

  • Endless hot water on demand
  • Lower monthly energy cost
  • Compact wall-mounted unit
  • Longer lifespan with upkeep

Households running two showers, a dishwasher, and a washing machine at the same time are the clearest case for tankless, since a tank simply runs out of stored hot water under that kind of simultaneous load. A single-person household with modest hot water needs often gets a perfectly good result from a standard tank at a lower upfront cost.

Before You Call: What To Check

  • Know where your main water shutoff is, in case a fitting needs to be isolated before work starts.
  • For a gas unit, note whether you smell gas near the tank. If you do, leave the house and call from outside before calling us.
  • Clear the area around the water heater so a technician has room to work and to maneuver the old unit out.
  • Have your home's approximate age and any past water heater service history ready. It speeds up sizing the right replacement.
