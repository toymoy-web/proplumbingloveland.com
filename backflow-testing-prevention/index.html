What This Actually Protects Against

Why Backflow Prevention Exists

Backflow is water flowing the wrong direction through the system, back toward the clean supply instead of forward toward the fixture. Under normal conditions this doesn't happen, but a sudden pressure drop, like a water main break nearby, can pull water backward through a connection. If that connection is an irrigation system that's been treated with fertilizer or pesticide, or any other cross-connection with a non-drinking-water source, backflow is how that contamination could end up pulled into the clean water supply. A backflow prevention assembly is what stops that from happening.

The annual testing requirement exists because a backflow preventer can fail quietly. Its internal check valves are mechanical parts that wear over time, and a valve that's stopped sealing properly gives no outward sign that anything's wrong until it's actually tested under pressure. That's different from most plumbing failures, which usually announce themselves with a leak, a smell, or a change in water flow. A backflow device can look and sound completely normal while no longer doing its job, which is the whole reason the test is required annually rather than left to whenever something seems off.

Missing the testing window isn't just a compliance issue either. Some water utilities can restrict or shut off service to a property that lets its backflow certification lapse, since the utility is ultimately responsible for the safety of the broader water supply the connection feeds into. Testing itself is performed by a technician certified to the ASSE cross-connection control testing standard, which is what most municipalities require for results to be accepted.

What To Do If You Suspect A Backflow Failure

  • Stop using the affected irrigation zone or connection until it's inspected.
  • Note any unusual taste, color, or odor in nearby drinking water and mention it when you call.
  • Do not attempt to disassemble or adjust the backflow assembly yourself.
  • Contact your water utility if you have any concern the drinking water supply itself may be affected.

Testing Vs. Installation

What We Actually Do

Annual testing checks whether an existing backflow prevention assembly's internal check valves are still holding and closing properly, since a device can fail quietly over time without any visible sign. New installation is a separate service, most commonly needed when a new irrigation system goes in, sized and installed to the local code requirement for that type of connection. A failed test usually means a repair or, if the device is old enough, a straightforward replacement.

Irrigation-Heavy Neighborhoods

Backflow tests are triggered by irrigation systems and certain commercial connections, so they cluster where lawns are big. Homes in Mariana Butte and around Boyd Lake with large irrigated lots, and commercial properties across Centerra, are the usual candidates. If you moved into a home with an existing sprinkler system, ask when the device was last tested.

Notices And Deadlines

What To Do When The Water Company Sends A Letter

Most backflow calls start with a notice. The water provider asks for an annual test, a certification is overdue, or valves were locked because paperwork was missed. Have the notice, the device location, and the account address ready.

Scheduling An Annual Test

Schedule early. Providers set deadlines and want the test report in a specific form. Ask the dispatcher what the visit includes, whether the report is filed for you, and what happens if the device fails.

When Valves Were Locked

A locked valve usually means a missed test or certification. The fix is to test the device, repair it if needed, and file the passing report. Start with the notice and its reference number.

Several Devices On One Property

Properties with more than one assembly, or an old preventer that needs removal and replacement, are quoted by count and condition. Photograph every assembly and note its size. Business properties often fall under the commercial plumbing page.
