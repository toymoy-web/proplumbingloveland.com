Tub-To-Shower Conversions

A Common, Practical Upgrade

Converting a bathtub into a walk-in shower is one of the more common calls we get, usually from homeowners prioritizing easier, safer access over a soaking tub they rarely use. The plumbing side involves relocating the drain and supply lines to fit the new shower base and adjusting the valve height and placement, in addition to whatever tile or surround work happens alongside it. We handle the plumbing portion of this directly and coordinate with other trades when it's part of a larger remodel.

Accessibility is a common driver behind this specific upgrade, and it's worth planning for early rather than as an afterthought. A curbless or low-threshold shower base, a properly positioned grab bar, and a valve placed within easy reach are all decisions that are far easier to build in during the initial installation than to retrofit later. If aging in place is part of the reason for the conversion, mentioning that upfront changes some of the layout choices we'd otherwise make by default. Most conversions still fit inside the existing tub's footprint, which keeps the job to plumbing and surround work rather than a full room reconfiguration.

Leaks And Failures

What's Actually Wrong With An Existing Unit

A leaking shower pan is usually either a failed seal at the drain connection, which is a straightforward repair, or cracking in the pan material itself, which typically means replacement rather than a patch. Low pressure specifically at the showerhead, rather than throughout the house, usually points to the shower valve or its supply line rather than anything upstream, and is worth diagnosing separately from a whole-house pressure issue.

Material choice matters more on a shower pan than most homeowners expect going in. Fiberglass and acrylic pans are lighter and less expensive but can flex and crack over time, while a tiled mortar-bed pan holds up longer but adds cost and installation time. We'll talk through which fits the budget and how long the fixture needs to last before recommending one over the other. Drain and slope requirements for either pan type still follow the same plumbing code standard the city inspects new installations against.

Remodel Season

Tub-To-Shower Conversions In Older Neighborhoods

Tub-to-shower conversions are popular in older homes near downtown and North Lake, where original tubs may date from the 1950s or earlier and the bathrooms are small. Newer primary-bath remodels in Mariana Butte more often involve larger walk-in showers with glass enclosures, which need correct slope, waterproofing, and a drain sized for the flow.

Tubs And Jets

Installing A New Tub And Servicing A Jetted One

Callers ask for a new tub in an existing bathroom as often as a full conversion. Jetted and whirlpool tubs add a pump and jets that need their own diagnosis. Share the make, whether the pump runs, and whether the leak is at the tub or its plumbing connection. A dripping tub or shower valve alone may fit the faucet and sink page better.
