Faucet Vs. Under-Sink Leak

Two Different Problems, Two Different Fixes

A dripping faucet and a leak under the sink are often treated as the same problem, but they usually aren't. A drip at the spout is almost always a worn cartridge or washer inside the faucet body itself. A puddle under the sink more often traces back to a loose or corroded supply line connection, a failing P-trap, or a shutoff valve that's started to weep, none of which have anything to do with the faucet's internal parts. Sorting out which one you actually have changes what gets repaired.

The shutoff valves themselves are worth a specific mention, since they're one of the more overlooked parts of a kitchen or bathroom sink. A valve that hasn't been turned in years can seize or start weeping the first time it's actually operated, which sometimes turns a simple faucet swap into an unplanned valve replacement. We check those valves as a standard part of any faucet job rather than assuming they'll cooperate.

Hard Water's Role

Why Cartridges Wear Faster Here

Front Range mineral content builds up inside a faucet cartridge and aerator faster than in soft-water regions, which shows up as reduced flow long before the cartridge fails completely. Regularly cleaning or replacing the aerator slows this down, but a cartridge that's already restricting flow usually needs to be swapped rather than cleaned at that point. Kitchen faucets tend to show this first, since they see more daily cycles than a bathroom sink, so a kitchen faucet losing pressure while the bathroom sinks still run fine is a pattern worth mentioning when you call.

Hard Water At The Tap

Why Faucets Scale Up Across Loveland

Front Range hard water leaves mineral buildup inside faucet cartridges and aerators, which is why drips and weak flow show up in homes from the older core near downtown to the newer Lakes at Centerra. A scaled aerator is a five-minute fix, while a worn cartridge or a corroded supply line under the sink is the more common reason a faucet keeps dripping.

Valves And Shutoffs

Stripped Knobs, Failed Valves, And Hoses That Will Not Come Off

Many faucet calls are really about the part behind the faucet. A stripped handle, a cartridge that will not shut off, or a hose seized onto an outdoor spigot each needs a different fix than a worn washer.

Stripped Knob Or Cartridge

The water will not shut off fully and the handle spins loose.

Dripping Laundry Valve

A slow leak behind the washer stays hidden until the floor is damaged.

Outdoor Spigot Will Not Turn

A seized handle or a hose fused to the thread needs careful removal.

Shower Valve Drips

A drip at the showerhead may start at the valve inside the wall.

Brands And Replacement Parts

Imported and designer fixtures often use proprietary cartridges. Share the brand and a photo of the handle when you call so the right part is sourced before the visit. A fixture you bought online is fine to bring, and the visit price should be explained separately from the cost of the fixture.

Before The Freeze

New hose bibs, extra outdoor faucets, and winterizing tend to be ordered together. Ask about frost-proof spigots when you are adding more than one. A spigot that leaks inside the wall is a burst pipe risk once temperatures drop. Tub and shower valve swaps may belong with the shower and tub page.
