Two Different Housing Eras

Downtown Berthoud Vs. The New Growth

Berthoud's population has grown by roughly 182% since 2000, and that growth shows up directly in the plumbing calls we get. The historic downtown core, with its early-1900s buildings along Mountain Avenue, sits alongside homes with plumbing that's never been fully updated, still running galvanized supply lines or original cast iron drains in some cases. The newer construction that's driven most of that population growth runs modern PEX and copper. Its calls tend to be about fixtures, water heaters, and appliance connections rather than pipe material itself failing.

The Berthoud Pioneer Museum downtown and the historic buildings around it give a sense of just how old some of this housing stock actually is, well over a century in places, and that age matters directly for what a technician expects to find on arrival. A home built before the town's growth boom is far more likely to still be on its original supply lines than a comparable home in one of the newer subdivisions built in just the last decade or two. Fickel Park and Waggener Farm Park are both real, well-used recreational landmarks in town, and the housing immediately around each varies block by block between older infill and newer construction rather than falling neatly into one era or the other.

Berthoud falls within the Thompson School District R-2J, the same district that serves Loveland, which is one more sign of how closely tied the two towns are day to day even though Berthoud keeps its own separate identity and downtown. Household income in Berthoud has climbed sharply along with the population, now running well above the statewide average, which tracks with the amount of higher-end new construction we see going up on the town's edges.

Winter freeze-thaw cycles hit Berthoud's older downtown homes harder than the newer subdivisions, since exposed or poorly insulated pipe runs from a century-old build are exactly the kind of thing that splits during a hard cold snap. A newer home's pipe runs are typically better insulated to current code from the start, which is part of why burst-pipe calls in Berthoud skew toward the older side of town even though the newer areas make up most of the population today.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Berthoud

  • Shut off your home's main water valve first, before doing anything else.
  • If water is anywhere near an outlet, switch, or panel, avoid that area and don't touch anything electrical there.
  • Move anything valuable away from the wet area if it's safe to do so.
