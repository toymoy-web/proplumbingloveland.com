Common Problems

Signs Your Disposal Needs Attention

Humming, Not Spinning

Motor runs but the flywheel doesn't turn, almost always a jam.

Leaking Underneath

Water pooling under the sink, usually a worn seal or cracked housing.

Won't Turn On At All

No sound at all, often a tripped reset button or wiring issue.

Foul Odor Or Slow Drain

Trapped food debris or a partial clog past the disposal chamber.

What Causes It

Why Disposals Jam Or Leak

A jam is almost always something the flywheel can't grind, fruit pits, bone fragments, fibrous produce like celery, or utensils that fall in by accident. The fix is usually a manual reset with the hex key that ships with most units, then clearing whatever caused the jam. Leaks are a different problem entirely. They come from the rubber seal around the sink flange breaking down over years of use, or from a cracked housing after the unit has taken a hard jam one too many times. We check both the flange seal and the housing itself before recommending anything, since a seal replacement is a much smaller job than a full unit swap.

Installation itself follows the drain-sizing and trap requirements set out in the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that Colorado has adopted, which is why a disposal can't just be plumbed into any existing drain line without checking trap arm length and venting first. That's a code detail homeowners rarely need to think about, but it's the difference between a disposal that drains properly for years and one that backs up within months.

One local wrinkle worth knowing: a meaningful share of Loveland's older homes near downtown still run cast iron drain lines rather than modern PVC, and cast iron narrows over decades as mineral scale and corrosion build up inside the pipe wall. A disposal that runs constantly into an already-narrowed cast iron line backs up more easily than the same unit draining into PVC. That's why a disposal problem in an older home sometimes turns out to be a drain-line problem once we get a camera in the line. It's worth mentioning on the phone if your home was built before the 1970s.

Repair Vs. Replace

When A New Unit Makes More Sense

Most jams and single leaks are worth repairing, especially on a unit under five years old. Once a disposal has failed more than once in a year, or the motor itself has burned out (a sign is a reset button that trips repeatedly with no jam present), replacement is usually the better value. We stock common residential horsepower ratings and can typically complete a swap in the same visit as the diagnosis.

Kitchens Across Loveland

Disposals In Older Bungalows And Newer Kitchens

Kitchens in older West Central Loveland homes often still have the sink drain layout they were built with, which can make a new disposal fit tighter than expected. Newer kitchens in Centerra and the Boyd Lake neighborhoods tend to have deeper sinks and heavier daily use. A jam, a hum with no grinding, or a leak at the housing is the same repair in either house, but the fit and the drain connections are not.

Before You Replace It

Leaks From The Back, Dead Units, And Reconnecting Lines

A disposal that leaks from the back or bottom usually has a failed seal or a cracked housing, so replacement is likely. A leak at the top flange or the drain connection may need only new seals. A unit that has power but will not run may be jammed.

Reinstalling After A Remodel

Sink swaps and countertop work leave disposals and dishwasher lines disconnected. Reattaching them involves the drain connection, the dishwasher hose, and a leak check at each joint. Say what was removed and what is new. A new sink faucet is a separate job on the faucet and sink page.

Backed Up Or Smelly

If water will not drain, the disposal may not be the cause. A clog past the P-trap is a drain problem, and the visit may turn into drain cleaning. Running cold water for a while after repairs prevents repeat jams.

Getting A Phone Estimate

A phone estimate is a range, because the model, the wiring, and the sink layout change the labor. Tell the dispatcher whether a unit already exists and whether you have bought the new one.
