How We Decide

Spot Repair Vs. Full Replacement

The same camera inspection used for a spot repair is what actually settles this question. If damage is isolated to a short section with sound pipe on either side, repairing just that section is the more cost-effective call. If the footage shows root intrusion, cracking, or corrosion spread across most of the line, replacing the whole lateral is usually the better call. The same is true when the line is old clay pipe that's already needed more than one repair. Replacing it avoids paying for repeated fixes on a line that's simply reached the end of its life.

Replacement Methods

Trenchless Pipe Bursting Vs. Traditional Dig

Trenchless pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old line's path while breaking the old pipe outward, using small access pits at each end instead of a full-length trench. It works well in most residential yards, though a line running under a driveway, mature trees, or other buried utilities sometimes rules it out. Traditional trenching replaces the line via a full-length excavation and is still the right call when site conditions make trenchless impractical.

Permitting is part of this either way. Because a sewer lateral connects directly to the public sewer main, the city treats replacement as work on shared infrastructure, not just a private repair, which is why an inspection sign-off is required before the line is buried and considered complete. Skipping that step to save time is the kind of shortcut that surfaces later as a real problem during a home sale, when a title company or buyer's inspector asks for permit records that don't exist and finds none on file. The replacement pipe material and slope still has to meet the same Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standard as a repair, permit or not.

Digging Or Not

Where Trenchless Replacement Matters Most

Trenchless replacement makes the biggest difference on the older blocks around Historic Downtown Loveland and Lake Loveland, where mature landscaping, sidewalks, and driveways sit right over the lateral. Newer neighborhoods in Centerra and the Boyd Lake area usually have newer PVC laterals that rarely need full replacement, so a camera inspection often turns a replacement quote into a spot repair.

Trenchless Questions

Lining, Bursting, And How Fast It Can Go

Owners searching for lateral replacement often use trenchless as a keyword. Lining coats the inside of an existing pipe with resin and needs a pipe that is still intact. Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old path and handles more damage. The camera findings and the entry points decide which one fits.

Lining Under A Slab

Lining a line that runs beneath a slab is worth asking about directly. Access from a cleanout is the key question, so tell the dispatcher where yours is. Smaller damage may be handled with a spot repair instead.

When There Is A Deadline

Some owners need the work done within days for a sale or a build schedule. Timing depends on locates, permits, and weather, so state your deadline up front and ask whether it is realistic.
