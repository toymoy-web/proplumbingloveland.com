Signs Of Hard Water

What Hard Water Actually Looks Like At Home

Spots On Dishes And Glass

Mineral deposits left behind after washing and drying.

Scale Around Fixtures

Chalky white buildup on faucets, showerheads, and drains.

Dry Skin And Hair

Minerals interfering with soap's ability to fully rinse away.

Appliances Failing Early

Water heaters and dishwashers wearing out faster than they should.

Why This Is Common Here

Loveland's Front Range Water

Front Range surface water carries enough dissolved calcium and magnesium to qualify as hard water across most of Loveland, which is exactly why scale buildup shows up repeatedly across other pages on this site. Tankless heaters, standard tanks, faucet cartridges, and garbage disposals all wear faster under the same mineral load. A softener addresses the actual cause across every one of those fixtures at once, rather than treating each symptom separately as it comes up.

The trade-off worth knowing about upfront is salt and water usage. A softener regenerates periodically, flushing accumulated minerals out of the resin bed using a brine solution, which uses both salt and a volume of water each cycle. A correctly sized unit keeps that cost reasonable relative to what it saves in appliance lifespan and reduced soap and cleaning product usage, while an oversized or poorly matched unit regenerates more often than it needs to for no real benefit.

We test actual hardness on-site rather than sizing off a general assumption about the area, since hardness can vary somewhat by neighborhood and source.

Repair Vs. New Install

What Determines The Right Fix

A softener that's stopped softening is usually a depleted or fouled resin bed, a salt bridge in the brine tank blocking regeneration, or a failed control valve, all of which are repairable on most units. Replacement makes more sense once a unit is old, undersized for the household's actual usage, or has failed more than once. Correct sizing matters as much as the repair-versus-replace decision. A unit too small for the household regenerates constantly and wears out faster, while an oversized one wastes salt and water on every cycle.

Same Water, Different Homes

Softener Sizing From Downtown To Centerra

Hard Front Range water affects an older home near downtown the same way it affects a newer one in The Lakes at Centerra. What changes is sizing. A small household in an older bungalow and a family of five in a large Mariana Butte home need different softener capacity, and we size by water hardness and daily water use.

Repair Or Install

Broken Valves, Cut Regeneration Lines, And Units You Already Bought

Callers fall into four groups. Some have a softener that stopped regenerating. Some have damage from movers or construction. Some bought equipment online and need an installer. Others want a check on a unit in a home they are buying.

Repairs After Damage

A cut regeneration line or a valve cracked when a tank was dropped is a damage repair, not a worn-out unit. Describe what happened and when the softener last worked.

Installing A Unit You Bought

A basement install needs a bypass, a drain connection, an overflow path, and a nearby outlet. Send the model and the grain capacity. Whether a specific brand is installed here is confirmed on the phone.

Conditioners And Other Systems

Water conditioners and descaler-style units are not the same as salt-based softeners. Ask whether yours can be serviced before you book. Brand coverage cannot be promised in advance.

Checking A Softener Before You Buy A House

A look at the unit age, the brine tank condition, salt use, and any leaks gives buyers a clearer picture. A hardness test is the way to confirm the system is working. If drinking water is the concern, see the filtration page.
